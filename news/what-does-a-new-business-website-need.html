What Does a New Business Website Actually Need?

By Zorn NovumOctober 26, 2026

A practical starting point for deciding what to put on your first business website—and what can wait.

A first business website needs to make a few things clear: what you offer, who it is for, and how someone can take the next step. Before choosing features or planning a large site, start with the questions a prospective customer would ask.

Explain what you do

Use straightforward language to describe your services or products. Give visitors enough detail to judge whether you can help with their particular need. A clear description of your offer is more useful than a broad promise to handle everything.

If you provide several services, group them in a way customers can understand. Distinct services may deserve their own pages when each has useful details to explain.

Make the next step easy to find

Decide what you want an interested visitor to do: ask a question, request a quote, arrange a conversation, or follow an appropriate purchase route. Give that action a clear label and place it where it makes sense.

For a service business, a short inquiry form can help you collect the information needed to assess a request. Ask only for what you need at that stage, and explain what happens after someone submits it.

Help people understand what to expect

Explain how a project begins and what you need from the customer. Where appropriate, describe your process, typical deliverables, and how the scope is agreed.

Use examples honestly. If an image is a design concept rather than completed customer work, label it accordingly. Clear expectations help visitors decide whether to get in touch.

Choose a useful starting size

A focused page can suit a business with one clear offer and one main action. A business with several distinct services may benefit from separate pages that answer different customer questions.

The right starting point depends on what visitors need to understand. Build enough to explain the offer well; add pages when they serve a clear purpose.

Check the everyday essentials

Before launch, open the site on a phone and a computer. Check that text is readable, navigation works, links lead to the right places, and forms can be completed successfully. If a form sends inquiries somewhere, verify that they arrive.

Know who manages the domain, hosting, content changes, and ongoing upkeep. Agree those responsibilities and any recurring costs before launch.

Decide what can wait

Customer accounts, booking systems, dashboards, and other integrations can be useful when they support a defined need. They also introduce requirements for setup, testing, maintenance, and support.

For each proposed feature, ask: what problem does this solve now? If there is no clear answer yet, keep it on a later-project list.

A short checklist for your first website

  • A clear explanation of your offer and intended customer.
  • Enough service detail to support an informed inquiry.
  • One obvious next step.
  • Working contact details or an inquiry form.
  • A readable experience on phones and computers.
  • Agreed responsibilities for hosting and upkeep.

Start with the information and actions your customers need most. A first website should give your business a useful foundation, with additional pages and functions scoped as the need becomes clearer.
