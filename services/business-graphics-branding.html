What the proposal defines

Know what will be delivered.

  • The named graphics or presentation deliverables, with sizes, formats and intended uses agreed in the brief.

  • The final files listed in the proposal, including editable files only where agreed, with commercial-use rights and licensing explained.

Deliverables, included revisions, acceptance criteria, third-party costs and recurring charges are specified in your proposal. Usage rights, ownership, licensing and any reuse or exclusivity arrangements are explained in the agreement.

Scope boundaries

A package covers the assets and revisions listed in its brief. Additional concepts, formats, sizes or designs may require a scope change. Original or template-based work, licensing, editable files and any exclusivity are agreed for the project. We do not promise blanket exclusive ownership of every component or unlimited revisions.

Before you inquire

Common questions

Can you work with an existing brand?

The brief can specify an existing brand direction and supplied assets. We agree which elements to follow, what needs to be created and whether the supplied material is permitted for the intended use.

Are editable files and exclusive rights included?

Only where agreed. The proposal identifies deliverables, editable files, commercial-use rights, licensing and any reuse or exclusivity arrangements for the particular work.

When do I pay for a graphics package?

Graphics packages and individual deliverables are paid in full before creative work begins. Larger projects use agreed stages paid before each begins. Monthly graphics fees are paid in advance; cancellation, refund and delivery terms are explained before payment.

How many revisions do I get?

The design brief and agreement specify the review process and included revisions before creation. Further revisions or additional assets are scoped separately where needed.

Tell us what you want to improve.

Describe the need, how you handle it today and who would use the result. Please do not include passwords, credentials, sensitive files or private customer records.
